\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

This Research Internship is part of the early validation work of the HybridNeuroPerception project. Its goal is to establish basic frame-based and event-based vision workflows on embedded platforms and verify real-time event data transfer between Kria and Jetson. On the NVIDIA Jetson Orin, a frame-camera baseline was built with \texttt{jetson-inference}, a C++ Intersection over Union (IoU) tracker, and a basic KITTI detection evaluation. The Recurrent Vision Transformers (RVT) model was then deployed for offline GEN1 validation and visualization of predictions and ground-truth annotations. The complete validation achieved a mean Average Precision (mAP) of 45.77\%, an AP50 of 71.06\%, and an AP75 of 48.80\%.

For the event-camera pipeline, the AMD Kria KV260 Vision AI Starter Kit and Prophesee IMX636 were used to acquire and display real event streams. The Metavision ROS 2 driver on the Kria publishes raw events as \texttt{EventPacket} messages to the Jetson. In Jetson Docker, \texttt{event\_camera\_codecs} and \texttt{event\_camera\_renderer} decode and render the events, and \texttt{rqt\_image\_view} displays the results on the host.

In the final tests, the Jetson continuously received the event stream. The stable \texttt{EventPacket} message rate was about 52--55 Hz. The observed throughput was mainly 11.6--14.2 MB/s in normal dynamic scenes, about 20 MB/s during fast motion under the current rate-limiting configuration, and 1.2--1.9 MB/s after the camera was covered. The renderer continuously produced event images. These results confirm that the distributed Kria--Jetson architecture can transfer, decode, render, and visualize real event streams, and that the event-data volume changes with scene dynamics. This provides a basis for later real-time event-based object detection and hardware optimization.

\textit{\textbf{Keywords: }Event camera, Embedded vision, Object detection, RVT, Kria KV260, NVIDIA Jetson, ROS 2}
